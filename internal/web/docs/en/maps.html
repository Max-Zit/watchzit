Network maps

A map is a drawing of your network that is also live monitoring: devices coloured by their state, cables that show traffic, ports you can zoom into.

Drawing a map

  1. Under Maps add a map and click Edit.
  2. Add device puts devices on the map; drag them into place. Add item adds things that are not monitored (patch panels, PDUs). While editing, Monitored device in an item's panel turns it into a device of the console: picture, place, rack unit and cables stay, and it shows the device's status and metrics. None (picture only) turns a device back into an item.
  3. Connect: click two devices and choose the ports at both ends (from SNMP or agent interfaces). The cable shows the traffic of that port.
  4. Suggested links finds neighbours over LLDP and MikroTik discovery and offers the cables with the right ports.
  5. Click Save (or Ctrl+S). Changes are kept only in the page until you save; Cancel goes back to the saved map.

Pictures, racks and cables

  • Every device has a picture from the built-in library (switches with their ports, routers, servers, firewalls, access points, phones, cameras …) or one you upload.
  • The library also has some 3,500 real models of over 180 manufacturers: servers (HPE ProLiant from G6, Dell PowerEdge, Lenovo, Supermicro, Cisco UCS, Fujitsu, Huawei/xFusion, Inspur), NAS (Synology, QNAP, Asustor, TrueNAS and others), SAN storage and FC switches (HPE MSA/3PAR/Nimble/Alletra, IBM Storwize/FlashSystem, Dell PowerVault/Unity/PowerStore, NetApp, Pure Storage, Lenovo, Fujitsu ETERNUS, Huawei OceanStor, Hitachi, Brocade, Cisco MDS), switches (Cisco, HPE Aruba, Dell, Juniper, Ubiquiti, Netgear, TP-Link, D-Link, Zyxel, Huawei, Arista, Extreme, Ruckus, Alcatel-Lucent, Ruijie, FS (fs.com: campus, PoE, data centre and industrial switches, Wi-Fi, media converters, fibre enclosures), QNAP, TRENDnet, Hikvision, Dahua, Lancom, DrayTek, industrial Moxa, Siemens SCALANCE, Hirschmann and Phoenix Contact, and others), firewalls (Fortinet, Palo Alto, Sophos, WatchGuard, SonicWall, Check Point, Cisco ASA/Firepower, Netgate and others), MikroTik, home and office routers and mesh Wi-Fi (TP-Link, ASUS, FRITZ!Box, Netgear, Linksys, D-Link, Huawei and ZTE ONTs and others), access points, UPSs (tower and rack), video recorders and IP cameras, outdoor point to point, sector and omni antennas, printers, IP phones and PBXs, blade chassis, PDUs, KVM and console servers, environment monitors and mini PCs, GPON OLTs and ONTs, service provider routers, SD-WAN edges, NTP time servers, intercoms, video conferencing, PLCs and HMIs. The generic pictures also have rack accessories: cable managers, brush panels, shelves, fan trays, drawers and blank panels. The generic pictures also have fibre patch panels (LC duplex single-mode and multimode, SC/APC and SC/UPC, 12 to 96 ports) and wall fibre boxes. In the picture panel choose the manufacturer (or All manufacturers) and the type (switch, router, firewall, server, storage / NAS, access point …), or search for the model (for example "DL380 Gen10" or "CRS326"); a type or a search with Generic pictures looks through all manufacturers; the drawings have the model's ports and drive bays, so cables end in the right port. For a device the panel opens with its manufacturer; when a device is added to the map and its SNMP description names a model of the library (MikroTik, for example), that picture is chosen by itself.
  • Racks of 4 to 48 units: drop devices into them; they snap to free units.
  • Cables have a kind (copper, fibre, DAC, wireless, VPN, WAN, serial, power) and a route: straight, 90° or 45° bends, curved; drag the points to bend them; double-click a point to remove it.
  • A background picture (a floor plan, a site) with size, transparency and position.
  • Submaps link to other maps and show their worst state.

Reading a map

  • Green is up, amber partly down, red down (blinking); grey means unknown or paused.
  • Cables get wider and change colour with utilisation (above 50 % amber, above 80 % red); a cable whose port has no link is red and dashed.
  • Zoom with the wheel or two fingers; names and port labels appear when you are close enough. Click a device for its panel with checks, ports and tools; double-click to open it.

HA groups

  • Devices that stand in for each other — a firewall HA pair, VRRP or HSRP routers, a switch stack or MLAG/vPC pair, a server or storage cluster — form a HA group: while editing, choose HA group → New HA group in the panel of the first device, then the same group in the panel of the others. The group is a dashed frame around its members; members mounted in a rack get only a thin outline and a small ⇅ HA tag where one meets the next, so the units around stay clear (click the tag to open the group); the VRRP role stands small beside the tag on each member — ACTIVE in green, STANDBY in amber. The group panel sets the name and kind.
  • Path goes through every member of a group and shows every equal route (for example 2 routes through two firewalls). Mark the cable between the members (heartbeat, sync, stack, peer link) with the cable kind HA link; it is not a way for traffic.
  • The frame (or tag) is green while all members are up, amber with redundancy lost when some are down and red when all are down. The trigger HA group without redundancy opens a problem then (see Alerts).
  • Members that report VRRP show ACTIVE or STANDBY, and the group panel says since when.

Hypervisors and virtual machines

  • Add hypervisor (while editing) adds a box for a virtualisation host: choose the hypervisor (VMware ESXi, Proxmox VE, Hyper-V, XCP-ng, KVM, Nutanix AHV, Docker, Kubernetes and others) and the physical server it runs on (required; for a cluster, add the other servers under Cluster, and paths can go through any of them); the server's state is shown on the box, its metrics open from the panel and, when the server is on the map, a dashed line joins them. Path goes from a virtual machine through its hypervisor and the physical server on along the cables.
  • Drag virtual machines into the box: devices of the console (to see their state) or items with a picture of their system (Windows Server, Ubuntu, Debian, Red Hat, MikroTik CHR, pfSense, containers and more, type Virtual machine in the picture panel). Inside the box they line up in a grid; drag them within it to change the order, out of it to take them off the host. A device's panel also has Runs on hypervisor.
  • Show the machines of the hypervisor by themselves (on by default for new boxes) fills the box with the machines a hypervisor check, the agent (Hyper-V, Docker) or vCenter reports for its physical server: running ones green, stopped ones grey, suspended ones yellow. A machine whose address or name is a device of the console shows as that device. These machines are not part of the saved map until you move or connect one, or press Place on the map; Place the machines on the map places all of them. Items you place yourself with the name of a machine get its state too.
  • The box shows the worst state of its virtual machines and of the physical server; the layer Hypervisors hides the boxes.

Picture library

Picture library in the menu keeps your own device pictures. Upload a picture (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or SVG; a front view works best) or take a built-in picture: the pencil changes the built-in picture itself, on every map where it is used (opening it changes nothing; a changed version is stored only when you save a change) (delete the changed picture to go back to the built-in one), and copy makes a new picture of your own from it, for a similar model. In the editor set:

  • Ports: click on the picture to add a port, port 1 first; drag a port to move it, double-click it to remove it. For a row, enter how many ports and click the first and the last one; in two lines numbers them like a switch, 1 above 2. The last SFP ports are drawn orange and suggest a fibre cable.
  • Rack units (0 when the device is not rack-mounted), manufacturer, type and the width on the map.
  • Copy makes a new picture from one of yours, for a similar model.

The pictures appear in the picture panel of the maps under their manufacturer and type, and cables on a map end in the ports you marked.

Layers and paths

  • Layers chooses what the map shows: devices, racks, items, submaps, cables, port names, traffic, VLAN labels, VXLAN tunnels, names and addresses, text and the background, in any combination. Your choice is kept in this browser.
  • Path: click two devices and the map shows only the chain of cables between them (with patch panels, switches and racks on the way) and zooms to it. Click Path again or press Esc to see the whole map.
  • Port names on cables appear when you zoom in, so a crowded map stays readable. They stand exactly where the cable ends: on the port of the picture, or where the cable leaves a picture without ports; they are drawn above all cables, so no cable hides them. A port that another cable already uses cannot be chosen again; the list says where its cable goes.
  • VLANs: when switches or routers on the map report VLANs, a VLAN list appears in the toolbar. Choose a VLAN and the map shows only the cables that carry it, marked tagged or untagged; devices without it fade. The cable panel lists the VLANs of its ports.
  • Cables are labelled with their VLAN mode, for example Access 20 or Trunk · native 1 · 10, 20-25 (layer VLAN labels). The VLAN filter can also show only tagged or only untagged VLANs. A cable is drawn dashed in orange when its ends disagree: an access port against a trunk, or another native VLAN on each end.
  • While editing, a cable's VLAN mode can be set by hand: Access with its VLAN, or Trunk with the native VLAN and the allowed (tagged) VLANs, ranges such as 20-25 included; Automatic uses what the ports report. A cable can also carry a VXLAN VNI, and the cable kind VXLAN tunnel draws a tunnel by hand.
  • VXLAN tunnels the devices report are drawn as dashed purple arcs with their VNI (layer VXLAN tunnels).

Kiosk displays

Under Settings → Kiosk displays create a display with the maps it shows and how often it changes between them. It gets a secret link that opens the maps full screen without signing in — for a monitoring TV. Delete the display to make the link stop working.
