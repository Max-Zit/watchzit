Network maps

A map is a drawing of your network that is also live monitoring: devices coloured by their state, cables that show traffic, ports you can zoom into.

Drawing a map

  1. Under Maps add a map and click Edit.
  2. Add device puts devices on the map; drag them into place. Add item adds things that are not monitored (patch panels, PDUs).
  3. Connect: click two devices and choose the ports at both ends (from SNMP or agent interfaces). The cable shows the traffic of that port.
  4. Suggested links finds neighbours over LLDP and MikroTik discovery and offers the cables with the right ports.
  5. Click Save (or Ctrl+S). Changes are kept only in the page until you save; Cancel goes back to the saved map.

Pictures, racks and cables

  • Every device has a picture from the built-in library (switches with their ports, routers, servers, firewalls, access points, phones, cameras …) or one you upload.
  • The library also has some 2,400 real models of about 90 manufacturers: servers (HPE ProLiant from G6, Dell PowerEdge, Lenovo, Supermicro, Cisco UCS, Fujitsu, Huawei/xFusion, Inspur), NAS (Synology, QNAP, Asustor, TrueNAS and others), SAN storage and FC switches (HPE MSA/3PAR/Nimble/Alletra, IBM Storwize/FlashSystem, Dell PowerVault/Unity/PowerStore, NetApp, Pure Storage, Lenovo, Fujitsu ETERNUS, Huawei OceanStor, Hitachi, Brocade, Cisco MDS), switches (Cisco, HPE Aruba, Dell, Juniper, Ubiquiti, Netgear, TP-Link, D-Link, Zyxel, Huawei, Arista, Extreme, Ruckus and others), firewalls (Fortinet, Palo Alto, Sophos, WatchGuard, SonicWall, Check Point, Cisco ASA/Firepower, Netgate and others), MikroTik, home and office routers and mesh Wi-Fi (TP-Link, ASUS, FRITZ!Box, Netgear, Linksys, D-Link, Huawei and ZTE ONTs and others), access points, rack UPSs and video recorders. The generic pictures also have fibre patch panels (LC duplex single-mode and multimode, SC/APC and SC/UPC, 12 to 96 ports) and wall fibre boxes. Choose the manufacturer in the picture panel or search for the model (for example "DL380 Gen10" or "CRS326"); the drawings have the model's ports and drive bays, so cables end in the right port.
  • Racks of 4 to 48 units: drop devices into them; they snap to free units.
  • Cables have a kind (copper, fibre, DAC, wireless, VPN, WAN, serial, power) and a route: straight, 90° or 45° bends, curved; drag the points to bend them; double-click a point to remove it.
  • A background picture (a floor plan, a site) with size, transparency and position.
  • Submaps link to other maps and show their worst state.

Reading a map

  • Green is up, amber partly down, red down (blinking); grey means unknown or paused.
  • Cables get wider and change colour with utilisation (above 50 % amber, above 80 % red); a cable whose port has no link is red and dashed.
  • Zoom with the wheel or two fingers; names and port labels appear when you are close enough. Click a device for its panel with checks, ports and tools; double-click to open it.

Layers and paths

  • Layers chooses what the map shows: devices, racks, items, submaps, cables, port names, traffic, VLAN labels, VXLAN tunnels, names and addresses, text and the background, in any combination. Your choice is kept in this browser.
  • Path: click two devices and the map shows only the chain of cables between them (with patch panels, switches and racks on the way) and zooms to it. Click Path again or press Esc to see the whole map.
  • Port names on cables appear when you zoom in, so a crowded map stays readable.
  • VLANs: when switches or routers on the map report VLANs, a VLAN list appears in the toolbar. Choose a VLAN and the map shows only the cables that carry it, marked tagged or untagged; devices without it fade. The cable panel lists the VLANs of its ports.
  • Cables are labelled with their VLAN mode, for example Access 20 or Trunk · native 1 · 10, 20-25 (layer VLAN labels). The VLAN filter can also show only tagged or only untagged VLANs. A cable is drawn dashed in orange when its ends disagree: an access port against a trunk, or another native VLAN on each end.
  • While editing, a cable's VLAN mode can be set by hand: Access with its VLAN, or Trunk with the native VLAN and the allowed (tagged) VLANs, ranges such as 20-25 included; Automatic uses what the ports report. A cable can also carry a VXLAN VNI, and the cable kind VXLAN tunnel draws a tunnel by hand.
  • VXLAN tunnels the devices report are drawn as dashed purple arcs with their VNI (layer VXLAN tunnels).

Kiosk displays

Under Settings → Kiosk displays create a display with the maps it shows and how often it changes between them. It gets a secret link that opens the maps full screen without signing in — for a monitoring TV. Delete the display to make the link stop working.
